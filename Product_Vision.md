# GeneoGraph Product Vision and Module Design

**Revision:** 2026-08-31  
**Document type:** Product vision and module concept  
**Audience:** Product designers, stakeholders, collaborators, and prospective users

## 1. Purpose of this document

This document explains the central idea of GeneoGraph, the value of each module, and the product’s long-term direction.

GeneoGraph is not positioned primarily as a specialist evidence-analysis service or a gateway to online historical records. Its purpose is broader and more personal: to give people one intuitive place to organize, connect, visualize, create, and preserve everything related to their family.

The document describes the product represented by the current prototype and prospective capabilities that could extend it. Prospective features, including AI features, are product directions rather than claims about functionality already available.

---

## 2. Product vision

GeneoGraph is a complete family workspace.

It brings together the family tree, people, photographs, videos, recorded interviews, documents, notes, places, visual boards, albums, and publications that normally end up scattered across many applications, folders, spreadsheets, and cloud services.

Traditional genealogy products usually begin and end with the family tree. GeneoGraph begins with the family tree but is not limited by it. A user can build a traditional tree, organize an inherited photo collection, keep personal memories, connect a recorded interview to the people it describes, design a custom visual family map, write a family book, or preserve a complete private archive.

The product’s central promise is:

> Keep your entire family world in one place, connect everything that belongs together, and turn it into something meaningful and beautiful.

GeneoGraph is built around five product pillars:

1. Organization
2. Interconnection
3. Visualization
4. Flexibility
5. Intuitivity

A sixth principle supports all five: users should be able to choose an online or offline way of working without losing the core GeneoGraph experience.

---

## 3. The problem GeneoGraph solves

Family information rarely exists in one form.

A typical family collection may include:

- a family tree on a genealogy website;
- photographs on phones, computers, external drives, and cloud accounts;
- certificates and scans in ordinary folders;
- interview recordings in a media application;
- memories in notebooks or text documents;
- lists and links in spreadsheets;
- family stories in unfinished documents;
- maps and timelines in presentation software;
- printed albums that have never been digitized;
- disconnected research and publishing projects.

The problem is not only that these materials are difficult to store. They are difficult to connect.

A birth certificate may belong to a person in the tree, mention two parents, refer to a place, and support a story in a family book. In a conventional workflow, the certificate, the tree entry, the notes, and the book draft may live in four different systems. The user has to remember the relationship between them or maintain a separate spreadsheet of links.

GeneoGraph replaces this fragmented workflow with one connected project.

## 4. Why GeneoGraph is different from a simple family tree editor

### A family tree editor is centered on relationships

It helps users create people, connect parents and children, and display generations. This remains an important part of GeneoGraph, but it represents only one part of family life.

### GeneoGraph is centered on the complete family collection

It gives equal importance to:

- people and relationships;
- photographs and albums;
- files and recorded media;
- personal notes and memories;
- places and journeys;
- visual exploration and custom diagrams;
- books, reports, and other finished creations.

### A family tree is one view, not the container for everything

The tree should remain clear and readable. It should not be forced to display every photograph, note, document, recording, and story at once. These materials live in dedicated modules while remaining connected to the same people and events.

### GeneoGraph supports creation as well as record keeping

Users are not restricted to an automatically generated family chart. They can arrange information visually, design a custom family tree, build a research or story board, curate albums, and prepare a family book.

### GeneoGraph gives users a storage choice

The application is envisioned as both an online and offline product. Users who value collaboration and access across devices can use online services. Users who value privacy, ownership, low cost, or independence from cloud access can keep their project and media locally.

## 5. Pillar one: Organization

### Product principle

Everything related to a family should have a clear home inside one project.

Organization is broader than creating a tree. GeneoGraph should help users manage:

- people;
- family relationships;
- photographs and albums;
- documents and folders;
- audio and video interviews;
- personal notes and memories;
- places and routes;
- visual boards;
- drafts and publications.

### User value

The user no longer needs to remember which application contains which part of the family story. The project becomes a personal family library that can grow gradually.

### Design consequences

- every major type of material has a dedicated module;
- modules share a familiar layout and interaction language;
- the project remembers recent work and important selections;
- search, sorting, filters, collections, favourites, and folders help large projects remain manageable;
- imported or newly added material can be organized later without blocking the user at the moment of capture;
- the system should remain useful for both a small family project and a large multigenerational archive.

### Prospective AI contribution

AI can reduce organizational work by suggesting titles, tags, dates, people, places, folders, albums, and related records. These remain suggestions that the user can review and change.

## 6. Pillar two: Interconnection

### Product principle

Every record can be connected to the people, places, events, and materials to which it belongs.

Interconnection removes the need for separate tracking tables and manual lists of links.

Examples include:

- a person connected to their birth record;
- a recorded interview connected to the speaker and the relatives discussed;
- a photograph connected to several people, a place, an approximate date, an album, and a note;
- a personal memory connected to a grandparent and a childhood home;
- a file connected to an event, a source, and a family-book chapter;
- a place connected to everyone who lived there;
- a Geneograph board connected to the people and media it visualizes.

### User value

Connections make information discoverable from whichever direction the user begins.

Someone can open a person and find their photographs, files, interviews, notes, and places. They can open a photograph and find the people in it. They can open a place and see the people and events connected to it.

### Design consequences

- a record should be reusable rather than copied into several modules;
- connecting something should be as natural as selecting a person or place;
- connected material should be visible in context without overwhelming the main view;
- removing a connection should not normally delete the underlying record;
- users should be able to follow connections and return to where they started;
- the product should support many-to-many connections, such as one interview involving several people.

### Prospective AI contribution

AI can suggest useful connections by recognizing names, faces, dates, places, and topics within the user’s own collection. A suggested connection should explain why it was proposed and should require confirmation before becoming part of the project.

## 7. Pillar three: Visualization

### Product principle

Family information should be understandable visually and adaptable to the user’s purpose.

Traditional genealogy tools often rely on a small set of automatically generated chart presets. GeneoGraph combines an automatic Family Tree with a flexible Geneograph canvas and a future publishing system.

### User value

Users can move from a clean automatic tree to a completely custom visual composition without leaving the project or recreating their people manually.

Visualization supports several needs:

- understanding relationships;
- exploring a complex family;
- presenting a family to relatives;
- investigating an open question;
- showing migration or place connections;
- combining people, photographs, text, and shapes;
- designing a unique chart or family-book page.

### Design consequences

- the automatic tree remains stable and readable as it grows;
- long names and translated text must not distort the tree layout;
- custom boards can contain people, places, photographs, text, notes, shapes, drawings, and connections;
- project records can be reused as visual objects;
- users can control arrangement, appearance, emphasis, and grouping;
- visual outputs should be suitable for both investigation and presentation;
- publishing should build on the same records instead of requiring a separate design workflow.

### Prospective AI contribution

AI can propose layouts, group related objects, create a starting composition, suggest a visual hierarchy, and adapt a board or tree for a chosen page size. It should assist design without replacing the user’s creative control.

## 8. Pillar four: Flexibility

### Product principle

GeneoGraph should adapt to different family projects instead of forcing every user into one genealogy workflow.

### User value

One person may want a traditional ancestor tree. Another may want to digitize family albums. Another may be writing a family book. Another may be preserving interviews and memories. Many users will do all of these over time.

GeneoGraph can support:

- a conventional family tree;
- a private family archive;
- a photo-album library;
- a family-book writing project;
- a collection of recorded interviews;
- a visual family map;
- a surname or locality project;
- an illustrated timeline;
- a custom family chart;
- a shared family memory collection.

### Design consequences

- users can begin with whichever material they already have;
- modules are valuable individually but become stronger when combined;
- optional features stay out of the way until they are needed;
- users can create their own collections, views, boards, and outputs;
- the application supports both structured information and free-form content;
- the product does not assume that every file or note must immediately become part of the formal family tree.

### Prospective AI contribution

AI can help transform the same collection into different outcomes: a photo story, a short biography, a family-book outline, a custom visual board, a timeline, or a summary for relatives.

## 9. Pillar five: Intuitivity

### Product principle

GeneoGraph should feel light, clear, and modern even when the project itself is complex.

Traditional genealogy services can accumulate dense screens, deeply nested menus, inconsistent controls, and specialist terminology. GeneoGraph aims to make common actions visible and understandable without requiring users to learn the entire system first.

### User value

A new user should be able to create a person, add a photograph, connect a file, or open a family branch without studying a manual. Experienced users should still have access to deeper organization and customization when required.

### Design consequences

- the interface reveals complexity progressively;
- important actions stay close to the item they affect;
- the same action should look and behave consistently across modules;
- quick inspection happens in side panels, while deeper editing opens only when needed;
- labels use familiar language;
- menus remain short and purposeful;
- icon-only controls are used where the meaning is clear and space is limited;
- empty states explain the next useful action;
- translations and longer names should not break layouts;
- the application should support keyboard navigation, readable focus, and accessible controls;
- offline and online modes should feel like the same product.

### Prospective AI contribution

AI can offer natural-language search, explain unfamiliar options, guide first-time users, and turn broad requests such as “make an album for Grandmother” into a reviewable starting point.

## 10. Core principle: Online and offline freedom

### Product vision

GeneoGraph is intended to support two complementary ways of working.

### Offline mode

In offline mode, the family project, photographs, recordings, and files are stored locally under the user’s control.

Offline mode is particularly valuable for:

- privacy-sensitive family collections;
- very large photo, video, or document libraries;
- users who do not want recurring cloud-storage costs;
- unreliable or limited internet connections;
- long-term ownership independent of a service subscription;
- regions where payment services, sanctions, availability, or data-policy restrictions make cloud products difficult to offer or use;
- archives and families that must keep data within a particular device or jurisdiction.

The core application should remain useful without a constant internet connection. Users should not lose essential organization, editing, visualization, or publishing capabilities simply because they work locally.

### Online mode

Online mode can add:

- access across devices;
- backup and recovery;
- family collaboration;
- controlled sharing;
- web publishing;
- optional cloud processing;
- easier contribution from relatives.

### Hybrid use

Users may want local ownership with selected online features. Examples include keeping original photographs locally while sharing a smaller family book, or maintaining a private master project while publishing selected branches.

### Product value

This dual model creates a credible free or low-cost path. Local storage avoids making the service pay indefinitely for every user’s high-volume media library. It also opens the product to markets where a cloud-only subscription is impractical.

### Important product requirement

Offline mode must be a maintained first-class experience, not an abandoned legacy companion to the online service. The product should avoid splitting into two incompatible applications with different features and project formats.

Specific public comparisons about competitor storage limits, pricing, or offline-product maintenance should be verified at the time of publication because those details can change.

Wants one understandable place for the tree, photographs, files, memories, and family stories.

### Family archivist

### Family storyteller

Wants to write a family book, prepare an anniversary presentation, build a photo story, or create a chart for relatives.

## 12. The connected workspace model

GeneoGraph uses three simple product levels.

### Projects

A project keeps one family or collection together. It can represent an extended family, one branch, a surname, a locality, a photo collection, or a family-book project.

### Records

People, relationships, places, photographs, files, interviews, notes, albums, and boards are the reusable items inside the project.

### Perspectives

Modules are different ways to work with the same items:

- Family Tree shows relationships;
- People provides an organized directory;
- Profile brings one person’s world together;
- Albums focuses on photographs;
- Archive focuses on files and recordings;
- Notes captures thoughts and stories;
- Places shows geography;
- Geneograph provides free-form visualization;
- Publish turns the collection into a finished creation.

The user should feel that they are moving around one connected family world, not switching between unrelated applications.

## 13. Projects

Projects are the containers for complete family collections.

A project gives the user a recognizable home, remembers recent work, and keeps unrelated collections separate.

### Contribution to the pillars

- **Organization:** contains the complete family collection.

### Prospective AI features

- create a useful project title and description from the user’s goal;
- suggest a starting organization for imported folders and media;
- produce a friendly project overview;
- help the user continue from where they stopped;
- create a starter project from a request such as “organize my grandmother’s photographs and interviews.”

## 14. Family Tree

### Core purpose

Family Tree provides an automatic, understandable view of family relationships.

### Main idea

The tree is the familiar center of genealogy, but it remains one perspective within the wider GeneoGraph project. It should be easy to navigate, quick to update, and stable as the family grows.

### Main features

- a focused person and surrounding relatives;
- parents, partners, children, siblings, and broader generations;
- clear cards with portraits, names, and essential dates;
- predictable spacing and connectors;
- fixed card sizes so long names do not distort the tree;
- placeholders for missing relatives;
- add and edit actions in context;
- focus history, pan, zoom, and generation controls;
- sensible display of current and maiden surnames.

### Contribution to the pillars

- **Organization:** keeps the family structure clear.
- **Interconnection:** opens each person’s photos, files, notes, and other material through Profile.
- **Visualization:** supplies a clean automatic tree.
- **Intuitivity:** supports direct manipulation without complex setup.

### Prospective AI features

- explain how two people are related;
- suggest likely duplicates for review;
- create a narrated tour of a family branch;
- propose a clean arrangement for unusual family structures;
- help convert an automatic tree into a custom Geneograph composition;
- prepare a selected branch for a poster, book, or presentation.

People is the organized directory of everyone in a project.

### Main idea

The tree is ideal for visual navigation, while People is better for finding, comparing, filtering, and managing records at scale.

### Main features

- search;
- sorting by name, dates, and activity;
- searchable surname and place filters;
- birth-year and living-status filters;
- configurable columns;
- quick side-panel inspection;
- multi-selection and bulk actions;
- saved views;
- relationship labels based on the current family structure;
- direct access to the full Profile.

### Contribution to the pillars

- **Organization:** turns a large tree into a manageable directory.
- **Interconnection:** reveals related media and records through each person.
- **Intuitivity:** supports familiar search, filter, and list patterns.

### Prospective AI features

- natural-language search such as “show everyone from Pawford born before 1950”;
- spelling-variant and duplicate suggestions;
- automatic groups for branches, households, or generations;

- smart lists such as “people without portraits” or “people mentioned in recent interviews.”

## 23. Search and discovery

### Core purpose

Search helps users find anything in the project regardless of its module.

### Product direction

The user should be able to search for a person, place, phrase, file, album, interview, note, or board from one consistent experience.

Results should make their type and context obvious. Opening a result should preserve a clear path back to the original search or connected record.

### Prospective AI features

## 24. Example user journeys

### Organizing an inherited family collection

1. Create a project for the family collection.
2. Add existing people or import a family tree.
3. Bring photographs into Albums and documents, recordings, and videos into Archive.
4. Create folders and albums that reflect the original collection.
5. Connect people, places, notes, and files as identities become known.
6. Keep the complete collection offline or enable selected online backup and sharing.

### Connecting a recorded interview

1. Add the audio or video recording to Archive.
2. Connect the speaker and people discussed in the interview.
3. Add relevant places and photographs.
4. Store interview notes or a transcript in the project.
5. Open a person’s Profile and find the interview among their connected material.
6. Use selected passages in a family book or visual story.

### Designing a custom family tree

1. Select a branch in Family Tree.
2. Open the people in a Geneograph board.
3. Arrange cards freely and add photographs, places, text, and decorative elements.
4. Adjust colors, shapes, connections, and emphasis.
5. Prepare the design for a poster, book spread, or presentation.

### Writing a family book

1. Create a collection of relevant people, photographs, notes, interviews, and places.
2. Plan chapters in Notes or on a Geneograph board.
3. Draft biographies and stories while opening connected records in context.
4. Choose the book scope and privacy settings in Publish.
5. Review the layout, captions, and included media.
6. Produce print and digital versions when publication generation is available.

### Building a private offline archive

1. Create a local project.
2. Store original high-resolution photos, recordings, and files on the user’s own device or chosen drive.
3. Organize and connect everything without a cloud-storage subscription.
4. Create local albums, boards, notes, and publications.
5. Export a complete preservation copy or selectively share chosen outputs.

## 25. Prospective AI assistant

AI should strengthen the five product pillars rather than redefine the product around AI.

### AI for organization

- suggest folders, albums, collections, titles, tags, and descriptions;
- group similar photographs and files;
- identify material that has not yet been organized;
- convert interviews and documents into searchable text.

### AI for interconnection

- suggest people who appear in photos or recordings;
- recognize names, places, and dates inside the user’s content;
- suggest links between files, notes, people, and places;
- explain why a connection may be useful.

### AI for visualization

- propose automatic layouts for custom trees and boards;
- group visual objects by family, place, time, or theme;
- adapt designs to different page and screen sizes;
- create alternative visual compositions for the user to choose from.

### AI for flexibility

- turn the same collection into a book outline, album story, presentation, map, timeline, or visual board;
- help transform rough notes and interviews into different formats;
- translate selected content for relatives in another language.

### AI for intuitivity

- provide natural-language search and commands;
- explain unfamiliar features;
- guide a first-time user through a goal instead of a long setup process;
- turn broad requests into reviewable starting points.

### User control

AI should prepare suggestions, drafts, and connections. It should not silently identify a person, change a family relationship, move or delete files, publish private information, or replace original content.

Every important suggestion should be reviewable and reversible.

### Offline AI direction

Where practical, privacy-sensitive tasks such as face grouping, transcription, metadata extraction, and semantic search should have local-processing options. More demanding online AI services can remain optional rather than making the core application dependent on cloud processing.

## 26. Market positioning

GeneoGraph positions itself between several existing product categories:

| Category | Typical strength | Common limitation GeneoGraph addresses |
|---|---|---|
| Family-tree services | People, relationships, hints, and sharing | The tree remains the center; broader files, thoughts, creative boards, and custom publishing may feel secondary or constrained |
| Cloud photo services | Photo storage and automatic grouping | Weak family relationships, document context, personal notes, and custom family outputs |
| File-storage services | Flexible folders and large files | Connections between people, places, photographs, stories, and family structure must be maintained manually |
| Note applications | Flexible writing and organization | Family records, media, places, and relationships are not native connected objects |
| Diagram tools | Free-form visualization | Users must recreate people and project information manually and keep diagrams synchronized themselves |
| Publishing tools | Strong page design | Family content must be copied out of the system where it is organized |

GeneoGraph’s advantage is not that each individual feature is unique. Its advantage is that organization, connection, visualization, creation, and local ownership exist within one coherent family product.

Services such as MyHeritage and Ancestry demonstrate the demand for family trees and family discovery. GeneoGraph differentiates itself by giving photos, files, recordings, notes, visual creation, and publishing a more central role, while offering an offline-first path that does not require every large media collection to occupy paid cloud storage.

Competitor names and specific claims should be used carefully in public materials. Storage allowances, prices, offline applications, and product maintenance can change and should be checked before publication.

## 27. Interface philosophy

### Clear before comprehensive

The first view should show what the user needs now. Advanced options should appear when relevant.

### One action, one familiar pattern

Sorting, filtering, connecting, editing, adding to collections, and confirming changes should work consistently across modules.

### Context without clutter

Side panels provide quick detail. Full pages and edit modes provide depth. The main workspace should not become overloaded with permanent controls.

### Visible common actions

Important actions should not be buried behind several nested menus. Menus remain useful for less frequent actions.

### Modern but not unfamiliar

The application can use contemporary layouts, cards, panels, and visual canvases while preserving familiar buttons, fields, lists, folders, albums, and navigation.

### Friendly to translated and personal content

Long names, localized text, and user-created descriptions should not break layouts. The interface should adapt without making the content feel secondary.

### The same experience online and offline

Users should not have to relearn the application when changing storage mode. Core modules, navigation, and project structure remain consistent.

## 28. Privacy, ownership, and portability

Family projects can contain highly personal photographs, recordings, documents, and information about living people.

GeneoGraph should follow these principles:

- the user owns and controls their project;
- local-only use is a complete product option;
- online sharing is explicit rather than automatic;
- living people receive clear privacy controls;
- originals are preserved when AI or editing creates enhanced copies;
- the user can export their family structure, files, media, notes, and project connections;
- important features are not designed to trap a user’s collection inside one subscription;
- local and cloud copies have understandable backup and conflict behavior;
- AI processing choices are visible, especially for faces, recordings, and private documents.

Portability is essential. A family archive may need to outlive a device, subscription, company, or current generation of users.

## 30. Product boundaries

GeneoGraph should not become:

- only another preset family-tree generator;
- a generic cloud drive with genealogy labels;
- a photo service that disconnects images from people and stories;
- a diagram tool that requires users to recreate their family data manually;
- a publishing tool that requires content to be copied out of the family project;
- a cloud-only subscription whose storage cost prevents users from preserving large collections;
- an offline application that receives only limited or legacy support;
- an AI system that takes control of private family content.

Its defining value comes from bringing family organization, interconnection, visualization, flexibility, intuitivity, and storage choice into one coherent experience.

## 31. Success measures

GeneoGraph is successful when users can:

- keep their family tree, photos, files, interviews, notes, and creative work in one project;
- find connected material without maintaining separate spreadsheets;
- move naturally from a person to their photographs, files, places, and stories;
- create a custom visual tree or board without specialist design skills;
- organize a photo or file collection without paying to upload every original to the cloud;
- work privately and reliably offline;
- enable online backup or sharing only when it adds value;
- turn the same collection into albums, books, posters, maps, and presentations;
- learn the application without navigating heavy, deeply nested menus;
- export and preserve their work independently;
- use AI assistance without giving up control of identities, files, privacy, or final output.

## 32. Summary

GeneoGraph is a complete family workspace rather than a traditional family-tree service.

Its five pillars define the product:

- **Organization:** keep the tree, people, photos, files, recordings, notes, places, boards, and publications together.
- **Interconnection:** connect every record so that people, media, places, memories, and creations can be explored from any direction.
- **Visualization:** combine a clear automatic family tree with powerful custom boards and publishing tools.
- **Flexibility:** support traditional genealogy, family archives, photo albums, recorded memories, custom trees, and family books in the same product.
- **Intuitivity:** make a complex family collection feel light, modern, consistent, and approachable.

Online and offline freedom completes the vision. Users can choose cloud access and collaboration, local privacy and low cost, or a hybrid of both. The offline product remains a maintained first-class experience rather than a reduced companion.

Prospective AI supports the pillars through organization, suggested connections, visual layouts, search, transcription, and creative drafting. It remains optional, reviewable, and compatible with a privacy-preserving local workflow.

The defining GeneoGraph experience is simple:

> One place for everything about your family—connected, visual, flexible, intuitive, and truly yours.
